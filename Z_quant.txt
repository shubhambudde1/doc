**Z-Score** is a statistical value that tells **how far a value is from the average (mean)** in terms of **standard deviations**.

Your code:

```python
data['zscore'] = ((data['Close'] - data['ma']) / data['std']).fillna(0)
```

### ✅ Formula of Z-Score

[
Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}
]

Where:

* **X** → Current value (`Close` price)
* **μ (mu)** → Mean / Average (`ma` = moving average)
* **σ (sigma)** → Standard deviation (`std`)

---

## ✅ Meaning in Stock Market Context

Here:

```python
Close = current stock price
ma    = moving average price
std   = price volatility
```

So Z-score tells:

👉 **How much current price deviates from normal price behavior**

---

## 📊 Interpretation

| Z-Score | Meaning                               |
| ------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 0       | Price = average                       |
| +1      | Price 1 std above average             |
| +2      | Price very high (possibly overbought) |
| -1      | Price below average                   |
| -2      | Price very low (possibly oversold)    |

---

## ✅ Example

Suppose:

```
Close price = 110
Moving Avg  = 100
Std Dev     = 5
```

```
Z = (110 - 100) / 5
Z = 2
```

➡ Price is **2 standard deviations above average**
➡ Stock may be **overvalued / mean reversion possible**

---

## ✅ Why Traders Use Z-Score

Common uses:

* Mean reversion strategies
* Detect overbought / oversold zones
* Statistical arbitrage
* Trading signals

Example rule:

```python
if zscore > 2:
    SELL
elif zscore < -2:
    BUY
```

---

## ✅ Why `.fillna(0)`?

At the beginning:

* Moving average or std may be `NaN`
* Division gives `NaN`

So:

```python
.fillna(0)
```

replaces missing values with **neutral score (0)**.
